SHOGUN is the drum computer of the Martial Systems Jidai Collection: fourteen drum voices and two note voices, a sequencer of 1 to 32 steps per track, and a patch bay styled like BUSHIDO's and RONIN's. The sound is the SHOGUN engine itself, compiled for the browser. TEST, next to the TRACK screen, plays the selected voice once. Tabs: RACK is the instrument: the face, the sequencer and, with BAY, the patch bay. LFO and VOICE replace it under the same top bar. Switching tabs never stops the sound or unplugs a cable. LFO: one tempo-synced LFO for the rack. DIVISION picks 1/1 to 1/32, with dotted and triplet 1/8 and 1/16; the rate is BPM / 60 × steps per beat, so 1/16 at 120 BPM is 8 Hz. It starts again from PHASE when the transport starts. SHAPE is sine, triangle, saw, square or sample and hold (a new random level each cycle). AMOUNT scales the output: at full it swings 0 to 5 V around 2.5 V, and at 0 it is 0 V. Its only output is the LFO OUT jack in the bay: patch it with a cable. VOICE: pick a voice and get large knobs for it alone, the same controls as on the face, plus BD 1 and CLAP TRANSIENT. One editor: the top bar, the knobs and the sequencer are always there. The BAY key opens the patch bay under the sequencer and the panel grows to hold it. Closing the bay shrinks the panel back and hides the jacks and cables; it does not unplug anything, and a patched cable keeps working. Voices: every voice is on the face with its name, its lamp and two rows of knobs. Click a voice's name to select its track (or use the TRACK screen); the lamp shows which track is selected. The open and closed hat share one TUNE. WAVE on BD 1, BD 2 and the toms is the folder on the body (0 is bypass). The toms each have a NOISE switch and a TOM/CGA switch; TOMS NOISE sets the noise level for all three. Sequencer: in TRACK, LENGTH sets the selected track from 1 to 32 steps, SHUFFLE delays its odd steps (16 settings), SHIFT moves the whole track later (up to 30 ms) and MUTE silences it. The 16 step keys are a page: 1-16 and 17-32 switch pages, and steps past the track's length are dimmed. Click a step key to turn it on or off. The ACC lamp under a step is its accent: dim green soft, green medium, red loud; click it to change. CLEAR empties the selected track. The last step key you clicked is the edit step (EDIT): FLAM picks one of 16 flams or none, BEND sets a pitch bend on BD 1, BD 2, the snare and the toms, and NOTE and TIE set the note and the tie on LEAD and BASS. The line under a step shows its flam and bend, or its note. SOLO lets only the selected track be heard; its lamp is lit while the selected track is the soloed one. A muted track stays silent. Solo is not saved with the pattern. Patterns: click the PATTERN screen for the list (the factory patterns, then your saved ones, up to 999), type to search, use the arrow keys or scroll, then click a row or press Enter. ◀ ▶ step through the list. SAVE writes over a saved pattern; on a factory pattern it asks for a name. COPY saves under a new name. Kits: KIT lists the sounds: loading one changes the knobs and keeps the steps. Chain: + adds the loaded pattern, − removes the last (Shift-click clears it), and clicking the CHAIN screen turns it on: each pattern then plays one bar, in order. UNDO takes back the last knob move or key press (Ctrl+Z; Shift-click or Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes). RANDOM rolls the selected voice's knobs. MIDI: notes 36 to 51 play the tracks in order. LEARN, then pick a track and hit a pad. Clock: TEMPO runs from 60 to 180 BPM, SCALE sets the steps per beat, BAR sets the bar length the STEP screen counts. The INT/EXT switch picks what fires the drums. In INT the pattern fires them and the Trig jacks are ignored. In EXT the pattern does not fire them; each drum fires only from its Trig jack, and the clock keeps counting. A cable into a Trig jack never moves the switch. Bay: each drum has a Trig in and an Out, under its name. The clock jacks are at the left: CLK IN (each pulse is one step), RST IN (back to step 1), RUN IN (start or stop), CLK OUT (a gate on every step) and ACC OUT (a gate on steps where a track has a loud accent). MASTER has Mix L and Mix R. The voice outs are taps before the master. The second row has LFO OUT and the CV inputs: BD1, BD2, SD and TOM PITCH, HAT DECAY and SD SNAPPY. A CV input adds to its knob: 5 V of pitch is about a fourth (1 V per semitone), and 5 V of HAT DECAY or SD SNAPPY is the whole knob, on the same decay curve. Drag from a jack to a jack, drag a plug to move it, drop it on empty space to unplug, click a patched jack for its stack. An input can take several cables and an output can feed several. Cable color is only for you. Hovering a jack or its name moves the cables aside. Knobs: drag up or down (Shift = fine), scroll, double-click resets. Esc cancels.
